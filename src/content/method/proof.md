Most prospecting emails get ignored because they talk about the product and the seller. The third part of the Repeat Success Method fixes that. Instead of describing what you sell, you describe what changed for a similar customer.

## What counts as proof

Proof is a short story with three pieces:

1. **A similar customer.** Same industry, ideally the same size.
2. **The challenge they had.** Something your buyer will recognize.
3. **A measurable result.** What changed, in a number you're approved to share.

If any piece is missing, it isn't proof yet. "We help companies like yours" is a claim. "A company like yours had this problem, changed this, and got this result" is proof.

## Build your library of proof

Don't wait until you're writing an email to go looking for a story. Build a library ahead of time, organized by industry. For each story, capture the industry and size, the challenge, what they changed and the approved result.

Start by asking marketing or customer success for approved customer stories. They often have more than you think. Then fill the gaps. If you have three accounts in an industry with no story to match, that's the next one to find.

## One rule that matters

> Never invent a number.

Use only results your company has approved you to share. Don't round up. Don't stretch a range to sound better. A buyer who catches one inflated number stops trusting the rest.

If you can't fill in the challenge and a measurable result, you aren't ready to send that email yet.

## Put it in the email

Every first email has three parts, and nothing else:

1. **Why you're writing to them.** One specific reason from your research.
2. **A similar customer, and what changed.** The challenge and a measurable result.
3. **A short ask.** 15 to 20 minutes, to see if you can repeat that success for them.

Here is the template. Fill in every bracket with something true:

> **Subject:** [Something specific to them]
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> I noticed [the specific reason]. [One sentence on why that matters.]
>
> [Similar customer] faced [the challenge]. By [what they changed], they [measurable result].
>
> Would you be open to 15 to 20 minutes to see if we can repeat that for [Company]?

Three or four sentences. No bullet points, no feature list. If it doesn't fit on a phone screen without scrolling, it's too long.

*A made-up example. The company, customer and numbers are fictional:*

> I saw Harlan Freight just opened a second warehouse. When another regional freight company did the same, their dispatchers were still planning routes by hand a year later. They moved route planning into one system, and missed delivery windows dropped from about one in five to one in twenty, with the same number of drivers. Would you be open to 15 to 20 minutes to see if we can repeat that for Harlan?

Every sentence gives a reason or gives proof.

## Use AI to draft it with you

Once you have your research and one approved story, AI can help you get to a first draft faster. Ask for two versions:

> I'm an AE selling [product] to [title] at [Company], a [industry] company of about [size]. Here is what I know about them: [paste your research]. Write a first email of 3 to 4 short sentences, no bullet points, in a warm, plain, peer-to-peer voice. (1) Open with the specific reason I'm reaching out. (2) Share one short result a similar [industry] customer saw: [paste your approved story, with the challenge and the result]. (3) Ask for 15 to 20 minutes to see if I can repeat that success. Do not list features or pitch the product. Use only the facts I've given you. Do not invent results or details about this person, and tell me if you need more information. Give me two versions.

Pick the better one and rewrite it in your own words. Then cut it down:

> Edit this email. Remove filler openers like "I hope this finds you well," cut adjectives, and make every sentence shorter. Keep the personal reason, the customer result and the 15 to 20 minute ask. Don't add anything new. Under 80 words.

Then read it out loud. If it doesn't sound like you, change it. AI tends to soften the ask and pile on adjectives, and the buyer can tell.

## One thing to do today

Write down the best customer story you have for the accounts on your list. Check that it has a challenge and a number you're approved to use. If it doesn't, that's the gap to close first.

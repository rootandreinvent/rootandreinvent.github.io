Every new-logo rep has more accounts than hours. The list is long, the quota is real, and the easy move is to start at the top of the alphabet or the top of the revenue column. Neither tells you who is ready to talk.

Prioritizing is the first part of the Repeat Success Method because everything after it depends on it. A great email sent to the wrong account is still the wrong email. The goal is simple: rank your accounts so one win can make your year, and decide who to call first.

## Rank by what changed, not by size

Big logos are tempting. They look good on a forecast and they feel like the right place to spend time. But size alone doesn't tell you anything about timing. A large company with no reason to change is a long, slow conversation.

> The best accounts to call are rarely the biggest. They are the ones that changed.

Change creates new priorities, new budgets and new people who want to make their mark. That is when a short, well-researched note has the best chance of landing.

## The signals that move an account up

Look for these. Any one of them is a reason to move an account up your list:

- **An acquisition, in either direction.** They bought a company, or someone bought them.
- **New private equity ownership.** New owners usually arrive with a plan, and the plan needs to show results.
- **A funding round.** New money comes with new expectations.
- **A deal that died last year.** Check your dead opportunities and the notes from the previous rep. The need may still be there.
- **A new leader in the seat.** New leaders often want to change how things are done.
- **A hiring push in a key team.** A wave of job postings tells you where they are investing.
- **Something the buyer said publicly about a priority.** If they said it out loud, it matters to them.

None of these guarantee a meeting. They give you a real reason to write, which is the first part of every good first email.

## Sort your accounts into four tiers

Once you know what to look for, score every account and sort them into **four tiers**.

Score each account on three things:

1. **Fit.** Does this company look like the customers you already help?
2. **Signals.** How many of the changes above are happening right now?
3. **Deal value.** If you win, does it move your number?

Put the top handful in **Tier 1**. These are the accounts where one win could make your year. Give them your best hours: your most focused research, your most personal outreach and your fastest follow-up.

The lower tiers still matter. They just get less of your time until something changes. When a Tier 3 account raises money or hires a new leader, it moves up. Your tiers are a living list, not a one-time exercise.

## Start with the accounts you already have

Before you hunt for brand-new names, look at what is already in your territory. Dead opportunities are one of the most overlooked places to find a deal. Someone already had a reason to talk to you once. Read the previous rep's notes, find out why it stalled and check whether anything has changed since.

If the champion left, there may be a new leader in the seat. If budget was the problem, there may be a funding round. A deal that died last year can be the easiest first meeting you book this year.

## Use AI to find your reason, carefully

AI can speed up the research. It can't replace your judgment. Ask it for public, citable facts only, and ask it to say when it can't find something. A prompt that works:

> I'm an AE selling [product] to [title] at [Company], a [industry] company of about [size]. Using only public information you can cite, tell me about recent events that might change their priorities: funding, acquisitions, leadership changes, new products or markets, and relevant job postings. Give a source for each. If you can't verify something, say "not found." Do not guess.

Then **check every fact**. Open the sources. If it found nothing real, stop.

> No personal reason means you aren't ready to send.

## One thing to do today

Pick ten accounts on your list. For each one, look for a single signal from the list above. Move the accounts with a real signal to the top. Those are your calls this week.
